# Product Doctrine

## 1. GoalMates reinforces a process
The product does more than remember tasks. It makes effective planning behavior easier to repeat: decide, act, close the loop, learn, adjust.

## 2. Paper is part of the system
A handwritten planner, whiteboard, grocery list, sketch, or scratch sheet is a valid GoalMates input. Digital capture should extend existing behavior rather than punish it.

## 3. Structure where structure pays; flexibility where context matters
Tasks, projects, schedules, rituals, ownership, and history are structured. Notes, research, inspiration, media, and decisions can be flexible.

## 4. Collaboration without managerial tone
Shared work can be pooled, claimed, requested, assigned where appropriate, or completed together. Language should favor coordination and offers of help over surveillance.

## 5. Plan realistically
A successful week is not the week with the most checkmarks. GoalMates should help users understand capacity, estimation error, over-planning, recurring friction, and what routines actually help.

## 6. AI augments; it does not own the product
Use AI/vision for capture, classification, summarization, suggestions, reconciliation assistance, and learning. Basic planning remains understandable and usable when AI is unavailable.

## 7. Progressive intelligence
Suggestions should improve as the system observes estimates vs actuals, planning behavior, completion patterns, contexts, and explicit preferences. Explanations should be available for consequential suggestions.

## 8. Rituals make the system durable
Daily, weekly, and monthly planning/review loops are product primitives. Inspired by proven habit/planning concepts, but do not copy proprietary planner content or wording.

## 9. History is useful data
Completed and superseded work feeds retrospectives and calibration. Default to preserving meaningful history rather than erasing it.

## 10. The interface serves the moment
Phone: immediate action. Tablet: planning together. Desktop: deeper organization. Do not merely scale one composition up and down.
